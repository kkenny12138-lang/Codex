# PRD Quality Checklist

## Problem and strategy

- The problem identifies a user, context, unmet outcome, and impact.
- Evidence is distinguishable from assumptions.
- “Why now” is credible.
- Goals describe outcomes; non-goals prevent scope drift.
- The proposal addresses the stated problem.

## Users and experience

- Primary users and use cases are explicit.
- The journey has a clear entry and completion.
- Relevant empty, loading, error, permission, recovery, and edge states are covered.
- Accessibility and localization are considered where relevant.

## Scope and requirements

- Current scope, non-goals, and future work are separate.
- Requirements use stable IDs and consistent priorities.
- Every must-have is specific and testable.
- Acceptance criteria describe observable behavior.
- Requirements do not contradict one another.
- Unnecessary architecture and visual prescriptions are absent.

## Measurement

- Every success metric maps to a goal.
- Metrics define population, window, and source where known.
- Targets are sourced or marked `TBD`.
- Guardrails cover plausible negative effects.
- Analytics and consent needs are captured.

## Delivery and risk

- Dependencies, constraints, and unknowns are visible.
- Applicable privacy, security, compliance, performance, reliability, and support needs are covered.
- Major risks have a mitigation or decision path.
- Rollout includes readiness, monitoring, and rollback conditions proportional to risk.

## Editorial integrity

- Facts, assumptions, recommendations, and open questions are distinct.
- Unknown owners, dates, and numbers are not invented.
- Vague adjectives are replaced with measurable or observable language.
- Boilerplate is removed.
- The summary agrees with the detailed scope and requirements.
